In one line

HUBIX is a Bucharest-based real estate investment platform that uses bulk buying power plus heavy due diligence to give small and mid-sized retail investors access to terms and returns normally available only to institutions and developers.

It runs two structurally different business lines. The distinction matters for anything built here, because the buyers, the legal wrapper, the money flow and the regulatory constraints are completely different.

Line A — HUBIX Hubs

Apartment aggregation & assignment resale

This is what the public site sells, and what 6 of the 7 documents describe.

The mechanic

  1. HUBIX screens developments and runs deep legal, urbanism and financial due diligence — over €13,000 of lawyer and economist cost per project, per the Roka document.
  2. It commits to a block of 20–40 apartments in one development and negotiates a volume discount off the developer's list price.
  3. It then assigns (cesiune) those pre-sale promises to individual retail buyers, at a price still below today's market, keeping a few percent of the volume discount as margin.
  4. All buyers in one building form a "HUB" — a collectively operated short- or mid-term rental cluster with a shared designer, shared furniture procurement, a shared property manager, and a private WhatsApp community.

"am negociat cu dezvoltatorul preturi discountate special pentru HUBIX (pentru volum)… din acest discount ne-am permis sa oferim toate avantajele clientilor finali, si sa mai ramana si HUBIX cu cateva procente."

Roka Park View prospectus — on the revenue model

Every other negotiated discount — furnishing, designer, property management — is passed through to the buyer rather than retained.

Money flow per buyer

  1. Reservation fee — €1,000 to €5,000Locks the unit. 1–2 days to decide.
  2. Tranche 1 = advance % + cesiune price + 19%/21% VATSigned at the notary. The cesiune price is HUBIX's margin.
  3. Tranche 2 / 3 = balance on deliveryPaid to the developer.

The cesiune price is the spread HUBIX negotiated. That is where the margin sits.

Line B — HUBIX Equity

Per-project co-investment SPVs

A separate and more interesting product — and the one with real regulatory shape.

  • HUBIX partners with a developer and buys 25–50% of a project or phase. Investors become direct shareholders in a per-project SPV (an SA, or an SRL for smaller deals) — deliberately not tokens, not fund units.
  • Board on every SPV: Bogdan Dinu (president), Cristian Istrătescu (VP), Daniel Tudor (member).
  • Target return: 42% over 3 years (~14%/yr) in EUR, net of company costs of roughly 9.1% of capital.
  • No management fee, no fixed compensation. Management is paid only the excess above the 42%/3yr hurdle. At 41% they get zero; at 43% they get 1%. Management also co-invests in every SPV.
  • Ticket: €10,000 minimum (RON 50,000 equivalent), practically €20,000. Above €100k requires a private conversation.
  • Maximum 149 privately invited subscribers per project — the Romanian private placement threshold. Expect 500–1,000 shareholders across 15–25 SPVs over two years, then the door closes.
  • Two asset classes: residential (build-to-sell) and retail parks (build-to-rent-to-turnkey-sell), the latter entered only with ≥80% pre-signed leases — typically 10+10 years, EUR-denominated, inflation-indexed.
  • Current state: 10 projects in development (6 residential, 4 retail). Pipeline: 6 resi + 4 retail in advanced negotiation, 5 resi + 2 retail earlier, plus Athens and Warsaw metro.
  • DD funnel: a 47-point pre-DD, then full legal, land, permits and financial DD. Around 200 projects analysed; just under 10% passed. They only enter deals where building permits are already issued.
  • Ongoing: monthly financial and quarterly technical audits by two distinct people — a finance person and an engineer. Shareholders are updated semi-annually.
  • Exit at project completion with full profit, or mid-project by selling shares, where existing shareholders hold statutory pre-emption. Or roll over into the next project, which compounds: 42% → 101.64% at six years → 186.33% at nine.
  • Deal information is deliberately withheld in advance — "sensitive and confidential". Projects are revealed one at a time with a subscription deadline, allocated first-come-first-served, with priority to people who filed an intent form and to existing Equity shareholders.

How they run this today

hubix.ro is a brochure site. Home page, terms, privacy, cookies, a newsletter form, ANPC badges. No login, no project pages, no inventory, no investor area. Every actual transaction happens outside it.

The real stack is Google Workspace and WhatsApp. Across the six project documents: 30 links to forms.gle, 16 to docs.google.com, 9 to drive.google.com. Concretely:

  • Prospectus — a Word or Google Doc, 13–20 pages, 1.7–8.6 MB with embedded renders, sent out per project.
  • Inventory — one Google Sheet per project, one row per apartment. Availability is a text cell: Disponibil? = DA / NU. Someone flips it by hand.
  • Reservation — a Google Form per project. Waitlists — separate forms per apartment type. Questions — yet another form.
  • The form's apartment dropdown is fed by a manually maintained helper column in the same sheet: Etaj 2 - Ap.203 - 2 camere → Ap.203.
  • The sales pipeline lives in hidden right-hand columns of the inventory sheet — Numar contract, Platit rezervare, Trimis rezervarea semnata, Cesiune semnata la NOTAR, Locatie cesiune, Comments Istratescu.
  • Reservation locking is a social convention, not a system. First-come-first-served if two people reserve at once; 1–2 days to decide; staff manually release the unit; a serial canceller is manually banned from reserving again.
  • Cancellations are free text in a comments column: Renuntare, Renuntare 2, renuntare 5, Nu a raspuns la mesaj si nici la apel, Nu are wapp si telefonul e inchis — and test / test2, still sitting there.
  • Post-sale community runs on WhatsApp, on a fixed countdown: −6 months design vote, −5 months supplier negotiation, −3 months property manager selection, then a handover party.
  • Equity side — one generic "Intenție Subscriere" form feeds a database; per-project documents are promised individually; allocation priority is applied by hand; semi-annual shareholder reporting is manual.

Symptoms visible in the files

  • Column layouts drift between projects. Roka has Transa 1 / Transa 3. Prima Solis has Transa 1 / Transa 2 plus two parking columns. Lake Home puts TVA redus? at column P; Cartierul Vienez at column M. Every project's sheet is a fresh hand-built variant, so nothing can be compared or aggregated across the portfolio.
  • A live file has a corrupted header. In #HUBIX _ Apartamente Cartierul Vienez TM.xlsx, cell A1 — which should read Nr Crt — instead reads Etaj 5 - M12_39A - Studio. Someone pasted over the header row and it is still there.
  • The Roka prospectus contradicts itself on the size of the block: "am ales sa achizitionam 20 de apartamente" in the description, then "cele 30 de apartamente achizitionate de HUBIX" in the contract section. With no single source of truth, the same fact is retyped into each document and drifts.
  • Availability is eventually-consistent at best. A DA in a spreadsheet a prospect opened this morning says nothing about a reservation taken an hour ago.

Constraints for anything built here

  • The Equity line cannot be a public listing. Maximum 149 privately invited subscribers per project, and they explicitly refuse to disclose deals in advance. Anything investor-facing on that side must be invitation-gated and access-logged, not browsable. Getting this wrong is a regulatory problem, not a UX one.
  • The two lines need different treatment. Line A is a semi-public sales funnel with real-time inventory. Line B is a private placement with subscription windows, allocation rules, capital calls and periodic reporting to shareholders.
  • GDPR is explicitly on their mind — the Equity document promises the intent database is used only for that purpose.
  • VAT is genuinely messy. 9% vs 19% vs 21%, buyer-type dependent, per unit — and it is a real column in the sheets. Any pricing engine has to model it, not assume it.
  • Money never flows through a platform today. Bank transfers, notary appointments, developer contracts. The realistic scope is orchestration and state tracking, not payments.
  • WhatsApp is deeply embedded in how they operate. Replacing it wholesale would be resisted; complementing it would not.

Where the biggest wins are

  1. There is no single source of truth for a project

    The prospectus, the inventory sheet, the reservation form dropdown, the waitlist forms and the admin pipeline are five separate artefacts kept in sync by hand, per project, six projects deep — and the syncing is visibly failing. A project entered once and rendered everywhere (investor page, live inventory, reservation flow, waitlist, admin pipeline, portfolio roll-up) removes most of that error class at a stroke.

  2. Reservation is an honour system

    Atomic locking with a real countdown timer, automatic release and automatic waitlist promotion is straightforward to build and directly protects revenue.
